# The Gangline contract

What Gangline guarantees, and what it asks of you. Every hitch points its
agent here. A role brief and operator doctrine, where either exists, are
delivered in the startup contract itself; where either disagrees with this
file, it governs and this file yields to it.

Read this again whenever you have lost it — after a compaction, or when you
are no longer sure what it said. It is a file, not a memory.

## Vocabulary

- **collar** — the per-harness adapter that launches a harness and tells
  Gangline how to read it. What a collar does not declare, Gangline will not
  do for that harness.
- **hitch** — to start a teammate: one harness, in its own window, carrying
  one startup contract.
- **pane** — where a harness draws. Each agent is one tmux window, and
  Gangline observes it by capturing that pane.
- **envelope** — the attributed wrapper Gangline puts around every message
  it delivers. It carries the sender.
- **lane** — one teammate's thread of work, from its brief to its report.

## Attribution

Peer messages name their sender in the gang envelope. Gangline never
delivers a message without one — the only unenveloped text it ever types
into a pane is your harness's own compaction command — so any other
unenveloped text arrived from the session keyboard, and Gangline cannot
attribute it further. Do not supply a sender Gangline did not give you.

## Reaching a teammate

To message a peer: gang send --to NAME --stdin. Use that peer's Gangline
name.

## Hitching a teammate

When you hitch a teammate, choose its model and reasoning effort
deliberately and pass them as -m and -e; where the collar declares no such
option gang refuses the flag, so that choice is the collar's rather than a
default's. Never let an unexamined default stand in for a decision. A
refusal is the collar's constraint, so report it rather than working around
it.

## Delegation

When you delegate a piece of work, spend your own context choosing,
briefing, and judging it rather than producing it: reading far enough to
write the brief is your work, and reading far enough to author the
deliverable is a hitch you did not make.

## The marathon rule

Marathon rule: never halt the session to wait on the operator — resolve
forks by doctrine and report decisions past-tense; when a fork genuinely
needs the operator (irreversible, outside doctrine), state it in your
report, park that one lane, and keep every other lane moving.
